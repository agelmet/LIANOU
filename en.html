About me

A few words about me

I am a psychologist, a graduate of the Department of Psychology of the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens, with a master’s degree in the “Treatment of Addictions” from the Athens Medical School. I am continuing my training in dramatherapy, steadily adding to my knowledge and my therapeutic tools.

I have worked with children, teenagers and adults, in one-to-one and group settings, designing and running psychoeducational, experiential and creative programmes. That experience taught me to fit my work to each person’s particular needs and their own pace.

In therapy I put particular weight on a relationship built on safety, acceptance, confidentiality and respect. Through talk and, where appropriate, experiential and creative means, I help you understand yourself better, put into words what is hard for you, and look for new ways to handle the challenges you face.

Dramatherapy

When it is easier to show it

Dramatherapy is a form of psychotherapy that uses the means of theatre — role, story, improvisation, movement, objects — inside a safe therapeutic frame. You do not need to know how to act: there is no audience, and no right or wrong.

Sometimes it is easier to show something than to say it. A role or a story gives the distance we need to come close to something difficult — and the room to try out a different way of being.

  • The role

    We try on a role, or look from a distance at one we already play. Whatever happens stays on the room’s stage.

  • The story

    A story, a myth or a fairy tale becomes a mirror: we talk about “them” and understand something about ourselves.

  • The scene

    A situation is set up and played again with another ending. Change is rehearsed here first, on safe ground.

In sessions, talk remains the foundation; dramatherapy’s means come in only where appropriate and at your own pace.

Self-esteem & self-knowledgeSelf-image

The voice that says “I am not enough” often sounds like the truth, when in fact it is something we learned. In therapy we listen to it up close: where it came from, what it protects, what it costs us. Together we work on self-image, boundaries and the ability to recognise your own needs. Self-knowledge is not a goal that “ends”, but a relationship with yourself that grows gentler.

Losing someone we love, a relationship, or a version of our life brings pain that follows no rules and no deadlines. What is dramatherapy?

It is a form of psychotherapy that uses the means of theatre — roles, stories, improvisation, movement, objects — to express and understand what is hard to say directly. It takes place in a safe therapeutic frame, with no audience. I am continuing my training in it and use it in sessions where appropriate.

Do I need to know how to act, or “perform” in front of someone?

No. Dramatherapy is not a performance and needs no theatre experience. There is no audience and no right or wrong. Nothing happens unless you want it to; talk always remains the basis of the session.

Do you see teenagers?

Yes. I hold one-to-one sessions with teenagers and with adults. With teenagers we work within a frame of confidentiality, through talk and, when it helps, creative ways of expression. For the first contact, the parent or the teenager can call.

Are sessions available online?

Yes. Sessions take place either in person at the practice in Ampelokipi or online by video call, with the same length and the same frame. All you need is a quiet place where you feel comfortable.

How long is a session?

Each session lasts 50 minutes, in person or online. We agree together at the start how often we meet, depending on what brings you and what suits you.

How do I pay? Can I cancel an appointment?

Payment is made at the practice, by card or in cash. You can cancel or move an appointment up to 24 hours before, by phone or through doctoranytime.

Where is the practice? Is there parking?

The practice is at 72 Papada Street, Ampelokipi 115 25, Athens, close to Katechaki Avenue and the Panormou and Katechaki metro stations (line 3), about a 10-minute walk. There is no private parking.

Does what I say stay between us?

Yes. Confidentiality is one of the foundations of the therapeutic relationship and binds every psychologist. Exceptions apply only when someone’s life or safety is seriously at risk — and I explain them openly from the first meeting.
